More leads can make a small firm worse

If you bill by the hour or by the matter, your growth has a ceiling, and the ceiling is you. Three attorneys can only take so much on at once.

So when an agency doubles your inquiries, here is what actually happens. Calls go unreturned for two days. Consultations get scheduled three weeks out. Matters already in progress move slower. And the reviews start saying the thing you never wanted them to say: hard to get a hold of.

You paid for that.

A contingency firm wants volume, because one case in forty pays for the other thirty-nine. Your practice is the opposite. You want the right matters, at the right fee, at a pace you can actually serve.

How we plan it

We start from your capacity and work backwards

Not from a package. Not from what we happen to sell. From the number you can handle.

01

How many matters can you take?

Per month, served properly, at the fee you want to be charging. This is the only number the whole plan is built on, and it is yours, not ours.

02

How many consultations is that?

If you sign one in three, six new matters means eighteen consultations. If your consultation is paid, that number also tells us what the filter has to do before it reaches your calendar.

03

How many people have to find you?

Working back from consultations to inquiries to visits gives us the traffic target. Usually it is far smaller than firms expect, which is good news: it is reachable.

04

Which channels produce exactly that?

Then, and only then, we pick. A local practice in one county does not need the same plan as one competing across a state. We build what hits the number and nothing else.

This is the part most proposals skip. If a plan arrives without asking what you can handle, it was written before anyone looked at your firm.

Before you ask

We're small. Is this worth it for us?

Small is the reason it works. The whole method is built around a capacity number, and a small number is easier to hit than a large one. What does not work is a small firm buying a plan designed for a large one.

We already tried an agency and it didn't do much.

Usually because the plan was the same one they sell everyone, and nobody checked whether your firm could absorb the result. Bring what they did. Half the call is often working out which parts were worth keeping.

How long before anything happens?

It depends which two or three things the plan starts with, which is exactly what the call decides. Anyone who gives you a timeline before seeing your firm is guessing.

Do you only work with one practice area?

No, but I don't treat them the same. Contingency practices get a different plan from flat-fee ones, because the economics are not comparable. That difference is the whole point.
