Tuesday, eleven at night

Someone gets hurt at work. That night, phone in bed, they search for a lawyer. They click your ad. You just paid for that click.

They land on your site, they find your contact form, and they start filling it in. Name. Email. Phone.

Then the form asks for the date of the accident. The insurance carrier. Whether they reported it in writing. Things nobody can answer at eleven at night from bed.

And they stop.

They don't come back tomorrow. They go back to the search results and click the firm underneath you.

The part that should bother you

You never find out they were there. No name, no email, nothing to follow up on. That person isn't in your CRM, isn't in a report, and isn't in any number anyone shows you at the end of the month. They're just gone.

On a contingency practice, one of those isn't a lost lead. It's a case.

Check it yourself tonight. Open your analytics. Count how many people reached your contact page last month, and how many requests you actually received. The gap between those two numbers is what this page is about, and it's your number, not mine.

This isn't a traffic problem

You already won the hard part. They found you, they clicked, they wanted to talk.

It's an intake problem. And it happens for a boring reason: your form was built to collect what the firm needs, in the order the firm wants it, from someone who is frightened, in pain, and typing with one thumb.

What RightLead does

It sits on top of the site you already have

One line of code. Nothing to rebuild, nothing to migrate.

01

It asks before it collects

It doesn't open with a form. It opens with one question: what happened. Everything after that depends on the answer, so a workplace injury is never asked about a car.

02

It keeps what they typed

Every answer is saved the moment it's typed. When someone leaves halfway, you still have their name, their email, and the exact step they stopped at.

03

It follows up without you

A reminder goes out on its own. When they come back, everything they already filled in is still there, so they finish instead of starting over.

04

It tells you where the case came from

Not just "Google". The campaign, and the page they read first, down to the blog post. So you know which spend is producing signed cases and which is producing clicks.

Before you ask

Do I have to change my website?

No. It's one line of code on the site you already have. Your pages, your design, your host. Nothing moves.

Won't this bring me more junk inquiries?

It should bring you fewer. The questions branch by case type, so someone with a matter you don't take gets filtered before they reach you, and the ones who do reach you arrive with the details already answered.

We already have a chat widget.

Most chat widgets start a conversation someone has to be there to answer, and keep nothing if the visitor leaves. This is the opposite: it works unattended, and its whole point is what happens to the people who don't finish.
